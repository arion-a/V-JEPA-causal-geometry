Protocol A pilot · frozen V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-L 384 · 2026-10-11

Does V-JEPA 2.1 encode gravity, bounce and friction as shared mechanisms?

Final pilot report. Every number comes from a file in the repository and a hash-chained ledger node (section 9). Equation labels (E1, E2, …) are used in the results tables.

Bottom line.
  • Confirmatory test (H2, held-out worlds, run once): not supported. V-JEPA's gravity direction is more alike across scenes than for raw pixels or an untrained network, but it is not distinguished from the tracked-trajectory baseline. Whole-frame pooling: +0.148 (97.5% interval −0.157 to 0.315). Object-window pooling: −0.031 (−0.218 to 0.133).
  • H1 (local sensitivity): supported for reliability, with a pooling caveat. Each parameter's feature change is repeatable across worlds; incline gravity is repeatable only with object-window pooling.
  • H3 (nuisance separation) and H4 (composition) were not analysed. The clips exist; the analysis was not run.
  • Exploratory Stage 7 (dev only): for a gravity change and an initial-speed change that look 75% alike on screen, V-JEPA's feature changes are noticeably less alike (cosine 0.60 whole-frame) than pixels (0.96) or trajectory (0.97). This hints at sensitivity to the cause, but early-frame speed cues and a residual mismatch are alternative explanations.

1. Question and hypotheses

Question: does a pretrained video encoder represent the physical generating mechanisms of a scene in a way that transfers across scenes, or only the trajectories and appearances they produce?

IDHypothesis (from protocol, section 1)Status
H1Changing gravity g, restitution e or friction μ gives a reliable, distinguishable change in the representation, where the parameter is observable.Reliability supported; distinguishability checked only as within-scene cosine (E12). Rank, metric and principal angles not computed.
H2Gravity-change directions align more across different scenes than matched baselines do. Primary, confirmatory.Not supported (section 6)
H3Physical-change and visual-nuisance (camera, colour) subspaces become more separable at some layers.Not tested
H4Latent change under simultaneous interventions equals the sum of the individual changes better than baselines predict.Not tested
NullApparent structure is explained by trajectory differences, image statistics, object identity or pooling choice.Not rejected

2. Definitions of key terms

V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-L 384
Frozen video encoder (official facebookresearch/vjepa2 commit 204698b), 304.7M parameters, 24 transformer blocks, width 1024. Input: 64 frames at 384×384, split into 16×16-pixel patches and 2-frame tubelets, giving 32×24×24 = 18,432 tokens. No weights were trained or changed.
World
One random draw of a scene's nuisance settings (object size, colour, start height, horizontal start offset and speed, slope angle), fixed by a seed. The world is the independent unit for all resampling.
Scene
One of three: freefall (no floor; g only), bounce (floor; g and restitution e), incline (slope then flat run-out; g and friction μ). Base values g = 9.8, e = 0.6, μ = 0.25.
Intervention step δ
Size of the parameter change on each side of the base: δg = 1.0, δe = 0.05, δμ = 0.009. Everything else about the world stays the same, so the pair differs only in that parameter.
Block-23 tap
The final transformer block's output after its own layer-norm. The analysis layer for the confirmatory test. Taps 5, 11, 17, 23 are the four hierarchical layers.
Global pooling
Mean over all 18,432 token vectors: one 1024-vector per clip.
Object-window pooling
Mean over only the tokens near the moving object. The window is the union, over all clips of a world, of the object's token path dilated by 2 tokens, so every clip of a world shares one window.
Derivative vector V
The difference in pooled features between the plus and minus clip of one world (E2). It is a secant over a step, not a tangent; directions can depend on step size.
Cosine
Angle-based similarity of two vectors, from −1 (opposite) through 0 (unrelated) to +1 (same direction). Magnitudes are ignored (E3).
Split-half reliability
How much the world-averaged derivative from one random half of the worlds agrees with the other half (E5). Near 0 means noise.
Noise ceiling
The highest agreement two noisy directions could show even if they were the same underlying direction (E7).
Cross-scene alignment G
Mean cosine of the gravity direction between pairs of scenes (E8). The primary statistic.
Trajectory baseline
The same calculation run on the simulator's tracked object positions (64×3 numbers per clip) instead of V-JEPA features. It answers: is the alignment just "things fall differently"?
Pixel baseline
The same calculation on 16 frames downsampled to 16×16 RGB.
Random-weight baseline
The same V-JEPA architecture with untrained random weights (seed 0), same clips and windows.
Wrong-parameter control
Compare gravity in one scene with a different parameter (e or μ) in the other. Gravity-specific alignment should beat it (E9).
Paired world bootstrap
Resample worlds with replacement within each scene, recompute a statistic, repeat 2,000 times, and read an interval off the percentiles. "Paired" means the same resampled worlds feed V-JEPA and every baseline (E10).

3. Design and conditions applied

3.1 Data

  • Simulator: PyBullet 3.2.7, DIRECT mode, TinyRenderer, deterministic and bit-identical across machines. 64 frames at 64 fps (1.0 s), physics step 1/256 s, 384 px.
  • Worlds: 30 per scene, split by index parity into 15 development (dev) and 15 held-out; the split was frozen before any analysis. Plus 10 nuisance and 10 composition worlds (not analysed).
  • Clips per world: base, g−, g+; bounce adds e−, e+; incline adds μ−, μ+.
  • Precision: fp32 only. bf16 was rejected after Gate 0 because it gave 1.0–1.75% relative error per block.

3.2 Validity conditions (a world is kept only if all hold, for every variant)

  • Object stays in view; freefall has no contact; bounce has ≥ 1 impact; incline slides ≥ 0.3 m and reaches the flat run-out between frame 12 and 50.
  • Peak pixel displacement between the plus and minus clip ≥ 2 px (amendment A5).
  • Rejected worlds are replaced by new worlds that keep the split; at most 400 rejections per scene.

3.3 Amendments made along the way (all logged, none after seeing held-out data)

  • A1–A5 (A1–A4 approved by the user; A5 applied within that approval and flagged to the user): object-window pooling as co-primary; 30 worlds per scene; incline redesigned to slope plus flat run-out so g and μ are separable (the first version made them exactly opposite, trajectory cosine −1.00); the 2 px validity threshold in place of 0.05 m.
  • A6 (user-approved): δg raised from 0.5 to 1.0 after the declared Gate 1c test (section 5.3).
  • Pixel baseline coarsened from 32×32 to 16×16 RGB to make the statistics fast.

4. The mathematics

Notation: c = a clip; w = a world; s = a scene; θ = (g, e, μ); E23 = the block-23 encoder output; n = number of worlds in a scene (15).

4.1 Features and derivatives

E1z(c) = pool( E₂₃( render(θ, w) ) ) ∈ ℝ¹⁰²⁴pool = mean over all tokens (global) or over the world's object window.
E2Vj(w) = z(θ + δj·ej, w) − z(θ − δj·ej, w), j ∈ {g, e, μ}A central difference without dividing by 2δ. The protocol divides by 2δ; division changes lengths only, so every cosine below is unaffected.
E3cos(a, b) = a·b / (‖a‖ ‖b‖) ũ = u / ‖u‖
E4V̄j,s = (1/n) Σw∈s Vj(w) (world-mean direction of parameter j in scene s)

4.2 Reliability and noise ceiling

E5r = mean over 2000 random splits (A, B), |A| = ⌊n/2⌋ of cos( V̄A, V̄B )Half sizes are 7 and 8 worlds. Fixed seed 0.
E6rfull = 2r / (1 + r)Spearman–Brown: converts half-sample reliability to the reliability of all 15 worlds. Undefined (shown as nan) when r ≤ 0.
E7ceiling(A, B) = √( rfull,A · rfull,B ) corrected score = observed / ceilingTwo directions measured with noise cannot show a cosine above this even if the true directions were identical. The ceiling is treated as a fixed number (its own uncertainty is not propagated).

4.3 The primary statistic

E8G = (1/3) Σ(A,B) cos( V̄g,A, V̄g,B ), pairs: freefall~bounce, freefall~incline, bounce~incline
E9W = (1/3) Σ(A,B) cos( V̄g,A, V̄p(B),B ), p(bounce) = e, p(incline) = μWrong-parameter version of E8. For the freefall~bounce pair the second scene's e replaces its g, and so on.
E10for b = 1..2000: draw world indices with replacement within each scene (same indices for every representation); compute G(b), W(b) for each representation; interval = [1.25th, 98.75th] percentile (97.5% two-sided)97.5% rather than 95% because there are two co-primary endpoints (global and window pooling): a Bonferroni split of 5%. The configuration text said "Holm"; the code uses this Bonferroni form, and the stricter version is what was run.

4.4 Hypothesis test and claim rule (frozen before held-out was opened)

E11Primary endpoint, for each pooling P ∈ {global, window}: ΔP = G(V-JEPA, P) − G(trajectory) SUCCESS(P) ⇔ lower 97.5% bound of ΔP(b) > 0 Controls: wrong_param_ok(P) ⇔ lower bound of ( G − W )(V-JEPA, P) > 0 baselines_ok(P) ⇔ lower bounds of ( G(pixel) − G(V-JEPA) ) ≤ 0 and ( G(random) − G(V-JEPA) ) ≤ 0 CLAIM "mechanism-like geometry" ⇔ SUCCESS(global) ∧ SUCCESS(window) ∧ wrong_param_ok ∧ baselines_ok (both poolings) One pooling succeeds → "pooling-dependent, not supported". Interval spans 0 → "not distinguished from kinematics". Upper bound < 0 → "trajectory baseline stronger".
E12Within-scene distinctness: cos( Vg(w), Vp(w) ) averaged over worlds, p = e (bounce) or μ (incline); 95% bootstrap interval.Near 0 → the two parameters move the representation in different directions.

4.5 Gate rule used to accept a parameter as measurable (Gate 1)

E13PASS(case) ⇔ r ≥ 0.5 at ≥ 2 of the 4 taps (5, 11, 17, 23), for at least one pooling; cases: g@freefall, g@bounce, g@incline, e@bounce, μ@incliner from E5, dev worlds only.

4.6 Stage 7: matching a gravity change with an initial-speed change

E14Free fall: z(t) = z₀ + v₀t − ½gt². A gravity change Δg gives Δzg(t) = −½Δg·t². Initial vertical speed v changes it by Δzv(t) = v·t. Least-squares match over the 64 frame times t: v* = Σt Δzg(t)·t / Σt t²For Δg = ±1 and a 1 s clip, v* = ∓(3/8)Δg·T = ∓0.375 m/s (simulated: 0.374).
E15Unmatched share = 1 − (∫t³)² / (∫t²·∫t⁴) = 1 − 15/16 = 1/16 of the energy → rms residual / rms effect = √(1/16) = 0.25A straight-line-in-time change can never copy a t² change. Measured: 0.249 in all 30 gravity/speed pairs. Declared acceptance limit: ≤ 0.30.
E16Cause-vs-effect cosine: C = cos( V̄g, V̄v ), Vg = z(g+) − z(g−), Vv = z(v+) − z(v−); corrected C = C / ceiling (E7); 95% paired world bootstrap. Reading fixed in advance: upper bound of corrected C < 0.8 → V-JEPA separates the causes beyond the visible trajectory; lower bound ≥ 0.9 → treats them alike; otherwise inconclusive.

Declared before running: test δg ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} by E13 on gravity cases. δg = 0.5 failed in bounce and incline; 1.0 is the smallest passing step; 2.0 pushed 9 of 30 free-fall variants out of frame. Adopted: 1.0 (A6, user approved).

6.3 Claim

"Not distinguished from kinematics." Neither interval clears zero, so the frozen rule gives no mechanism-like claim. The global-pooling estimate favours V-JEPA, the opposite sign from dev, which shows how unstable a 15-world comparison is.

6.4 Interpretation limits

  • The result neither shows V-JEPA equals the trajectory baseline nor that it beats it. The intervals are wide (about ±0.25).
  • The trajectory baseline's G depends on the scene geometry (gravity acts vertically in two scenes and along a slope in the third); it dropped from 0.30 on dev to 0.145 on held-out.
  • Held-out is now used up. The script refuses a second run.

Declared reading (E16): global upper bound 0.75 < 0.8 → V-JEPA separates the two causes beyond the visible trajectory; window upper bound 0.81 → inconclusive. Pixels and the trajectory say the two clips differ by a cosine of 0.96–0.97, so V-JEPA is picking up something that those two do not.

Why this is not a mechanism result:

  • A speed change is visible from the first frame; a gravity change accumulates. A feature that reads early speed would also give a low C.
  • The 25% unmatched trajectory could be amplified by the network.
  • Per-world cosines (0.38) were barely above cosines taken against another world (0.34), so only the world-mean statistic is interpretable.
  • One scene, 15 worlds, no held-out confirmation.

9. Limitations and deviations

  • Small samples. 15 worlds per scene per split. Intervals are wide and the dev and held-out point estimates disagreed in sign.
  • Secant, not tangent. A change of step size changes the measured direction. The gravity step was chosen by the repeatability gate on dev worlds, so it is tuned to make directions measurable.
  • Incline gravity is repeatable only with object-window pooling, and the window's own validity rests on the object-tracking positions from the simulator.
  • Bootstrap bias. Percentile intervals are biased low when the statistic is near 1. A sign-flip null showed reliability near 0 under no signal.
  • Ceiling treated as a point value; not propagated.
  • Process. Much of the early effort went into pod access (a git-based job worker, then a Jupyter terminal). A launch refusal on the held-out run came from a ledger check that listed files not in the pod's copy; no analysis choice changed.

11. Recommendation

Do not claim mechanism-level encoding of gravity from this pilot. The evidence supports "some shared gravity structure that pixels and an untrained network lack, not separable from trajectory information at this sample size". If the question is worth pursuing, the next study should (a) use at least 60 new worlds per scene with a fresh held-out set and a preregistered primary of cause-versus-effect matching (Stage 7 design, with matching that includes early-frame speed), and (b) analyse H3 and H4 on clips that already exist.
